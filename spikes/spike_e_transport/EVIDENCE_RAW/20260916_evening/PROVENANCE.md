### Overlay incident, same evening — why a process check is not a health check

Between **13:34 and 21:33** the Mac mini left ZeroTier network `b103a835d292ddb3` on its own: `zerotier-cli
listnetworks` listed only the old network and `10.64.193.115` was gone from every interface, while the stub
process stayed alive and `lsof` still reported it `LISTEN` on that address. Any check of the form "is the stub
running?" answered **yes** for about eight hours while nothing on the network — the phone included — could
reach it. Project Control re-joined the network (address back in 10 s; the membership was still authorised),
restarted the stub on the restored address, and re-verified both profiles before measuring. Recorded in
`RISK-DEMO-NET-01` and in the T−60 checklist of `management/DEMO_STANDARD.md` §8.

## Boundaries

- Payload bytes are **synthetic**: shapes and dtypes mirror the Spike D `A6` profiles, the bytes do not come
  from the cohort. This capture measures transport, never data quality.
- This is **not** an acceptance verdict. `SPIKE_E` stays `ACTIVE`; `evidence_present` turns true only when the
  owner's `RESULT.md` lands on `main`, and `ACCEPTED` needs reviewer approval plus the QA step.
- `E7` (app memory) and `E9` (reconnect/retry) are not in this capture: `E7` needs a real app, and `E9` is the
  owner's controlled drill (PR #33).
- The stub does **not** implement HTTP Range: a range request returns `200` with the whole body. Any strategy
  that assumes partial fetches has to be re-checked against that.
